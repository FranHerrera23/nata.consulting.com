Jura Plank

High-end flooring · Jacksonville & Miami, FLUS$1M annual revenue

The problem

The founder knew how to sell the floor. Collecting the details took the time. Each quote meant chasing specifications across emails, drawings and spreadsheets. More follow-up would have meant more of the same.

What we did

Changed how the information arrives. A guided, seven-step project registration collects the specifications; Close CRM organizes them for the team. Fix the starting point, and there is less to chase.

Business outcome

A clearer starting point for every quote, designed to give the founder more room to sell. The opportunity is time redirected toward customers and new business.

TRAZZO LIGHTING / Karen Mannheim

Lighting design · Residential, commercial & hospitality

Led by Karen Mannheim, TRAZZO collaborates with Robert A. M. Stern Architects, Kobi Karp and Debora Wecselman Design.

The problem

The lighting required judgment. The proposal looked like a supply list. Preparing a new pitch could take the team two or three days, assembling PowerPoint, Word and Excel files that left too much of the thinking unexplained.

What we did

Designed a reusable digital presentation system that connects the project brief, lighting intent, scenes and scope. A way to show the reasoning behind the recommendation—before asking the client to evaluate the price.

Business outcome

The expertise becomes part of the pitch. Clients can see how the studio thinks, what the lighting will bring to a space and why each recommendation belongs. A presentation that gives the design its full value before the conversation turns to price.

Our point of view

The problem crosses departments.
Your response should too.

Sales sees a delayed quote. Finance sees margin slipping. Operations sees another urgent request. You have to make sense of all three. Adding tools—or accelerating one task—can leave the relationship between them untouched.

Physicist Fritjof Capra’s systems thinking informs how we look: understand the relationships, patterns and context that shape what happens. As businesses add more technology, that perspective becomes especially useful. We listen across the business, find where a change can help, and test it with the people doing the work.
