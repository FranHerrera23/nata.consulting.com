TRAZZO LIGHTING / Karen Mannheim

Lighting design · Residential, commercial & hospitality

Led by Karen Mannheim, TRAZZO collaborates with Robert A. M. Stern Architects, Kobi Karp and Debora Wecselman Design.

The problem

The lighting required judgment. The proposal looked like a supply list. Preparing a new pitch could take the team two or three days, assembling PowerPoint, Word and Excel files that left too much of the thinking unexplained.

What we did

Designed a reusable digital presentation system that connects the project brief, lighting intent, scenes and scope. A way to show the reasoning behind the recommendation—before asking the client to evaluate the price.

Business outcome

The expertise becomes part of the pitch. Clients can see how the studio thinks, what the lighting will bring to a space and why each recommendation belongs. A presentation that gives the design its full value before the conversation turns to price.

Our point of view / Fritjof Capra

See the whole.
Find where to begin.

A change in perception.

Fritjof Capra’s The Turning Point (1982), known in Spanish as El punto crucial, helped frame an idea that resonates with us: how we see a problem shapes the answers we can imagine. The book explores a shift in worldview across science and society. His later work develops a systems view of life, grounded in relationships, patterns and context.

In that view, a living system cannot be understood through its parts alone. Its organisation emerges through their relationships. Feedback, communication and learning matter.

Further reading: The Turning Point · Principles of life

From that idea to everyday work.

For NATA, this becomes a practical way to enter a business. A delayed quote may begin with missing information. A support problem may start with a promise made in sales. We listen across those boundaries before deciding where to act.

Our creative background brings another habit: noticing relationships that are easy to overlook. Discovery comes through attention and conversation. A shared understanding makes a different decision possible.

We call that moment the point of change. Then we build one useful improvement with the people who will live with it, observe its effects and refine it.
