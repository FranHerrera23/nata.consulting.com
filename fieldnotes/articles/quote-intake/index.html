What Information Should a Materials Supplier Collect Before Preparing a Quote?

A quote request becomes useful when the team can tell what is known, what remains open, and who can resolve it.

Fran Herrera · Founder, NATA

Prepared and revised November 4, 2026

The first job of quote intake is to establish what kind of answer the customer needs. A preliminary budget, a product recommendation, and a firm quotation require different levels of certainty. Asking every customer for every possible detail can make an early conversation harder without making the eventual quote more reliable.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Establish whether the customer needs budget guidance or a firm quote.
  • Give uncertainty a status and a decision owner.
  • Investigate why information did not reach the point of use.

What kind of answer does the customer need from the quote?

Establish whether the request is for budget guidance, product advice, a firm quotation, or a revision. Those responses require different levels of certainty. An architect testing a project budget may still be choosing a finish; a purchaser preparing an order needs a confirmed basis for buying.

Write down the intended response before checking the documents: budget guidance, product options, priced scope, or a revision to an existing quote. Confirm who will use that response and what decision it supports. This prevents a preliminary figure from becoming an apparently settled commitment as it moves between people.

How should you record missing or conflicting quote information?

Record whether an input is confirmed, missing, undecided, conflicting, or not applicable. Each state needs a different next action: retrieve a fact, help make a choice, resolve a contradiction, or proceed without an unnecessary field. The NATA Quote Intake Review below makes those distinctions usable.

The team responsible for pricing determines sufficiency for the product and response requested. This worksheet does not set technical requirements for every material.

Who needs to be identified in a quote request?

Identify the sender, specifier, purchaser, and person responsible for site or delivery questions where those roles affect the quote. They may be different people. Record who can resolve each open decision so the team does not send a technical question to someone who only forwarded the request.

Which project details should a materials supplier check?

Check the scope, exclusions, quantity and unit, specification status, applicable document revisions, location, and relevant dates. Separate the quote deadline from sample or delivery dates. The required detail depends on the product and the answer requested; do not ask customers to supply information the company already holds.

A manufacturer’s requirements will depend on its product. BWG Steel Framing’s checklist is one published example of product-specific quote inputs. Its requirements should not become a universal form for unrelated materials.

What if a selection is undecided and the drawings disagree?

Treat an undecided selection and conflicting drawings as separate issues. The first may allow advice or an explicit provisional assumption; the second needs confirmation of the applicable source. In an illustrative flooring inquiry, a preliminary budget could progress only on a clearly stated basis while conflicting floor areas are resolved.

The unfinished selection calls for a decision about product guidance or an explicitly accepted assumption. The inconsistent quantities call for confirmation of the applicable document. A reminder asking the customer to “complete all details” fails to make either action clear.

The quoting lead can record the exact discrepancy, identify who resolves it, and decide whether any unaffected part can progress. The customer receives a bounded request rather than another general instruction to send more information.

How do you stop the same intake gaps from recurring?

Trace one recurring gap back to what the customer was told and what the company already knew. Check whether the instruction was clear, the decision was possible at that stage, or the information was lost internally. Test the smallest change that addresses the observed cause before adding another required field.

These questions produce hypotheses, not proof. Check an actual request and the material the customer saw before adding fields or automation. The smallest useful change might be a clearer product guide, a document-version instruction, or a named decision owner.

To review another request, record the intended answer, the unresolved item, its status, the responsible person, and the next decision. Then compare the source of the difficulty. The aim is a more useful start to the quote and a clearer experience for the person asking for it.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need every technical detail before giving an early budget?

The required certainty depends on the response. Preliminary guidance may be possible on a clearly stated basis, while a firm quotation needs the inputs the supplier considers sufficient. Do not silently present an assumption as a confirmed specification.

Should an inquiry form require every field?

Require a field when it is needed at that stage and the customer can reasonably answer it. Provide a route for an undecided selection or an early question. A long form can collect more entries without producing a more usable request.

Will a better intake form remove the need for sales advice?

No. It can organize facts and expose gaps. Helping a customer choose or assessing an unusual specification may still require a conversation with the right person.
